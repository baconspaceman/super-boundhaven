Owner direction (2026-09-30): "I still want it to look like Super Mario World — the bones, the essence, the inspiration." Every art decision (hand-pixeled or Blender-rendered) is judged against this page first. More detail and richer depth are welcome, but never at the cost of the essence below.

## The essence (what SMW actually does, which we keep)

1. **Bold, cheerful, instantly readable.** Saturated, friendly colors; sky-blue skies, bright greens, warm earth. Nothing muddy, nothing grim. A screenshot reads in half a second.
2. **Chunky, simple, strong shapes.** Big clear shapes first, detail second. Silhouettes are unmistakable. Clusters are clean; no noise.
3. **Dark, confident outlines on characters, creatures and props** (hue-matched dark, never pure black), so sprites pop off every background. Terrain is shaded with flat, hand-placed 2–3 tone blocks rather than soft gradients.
4. **Small, stout, expressive characters.** Roughly 2–3 heads tall, big readable heads/hands/feet, face with clear eyes. Humanoid (not blocks). Animation is few frames but each frame has strong posing: clear run lean, skid, crouch, spin/bounce; personality in idle.
5. **Flat-shaded 16x16 tile language.** Grass-capped ground with a bright top lip, layered earth, bricks, pipes/blocks-style solid pieces, semi-solid platforms, slopes that connect cleanly. One clear tileset rule: what you can stand on looks solid and high-contrast; decoration looks soft.
6. **Backgrounds with friendly depth.** Gentle layered hills/mountains/clouds in a clear sky, slightly lower contrast than the playfield, gentle parallax; each world has an instantly identifiable theme color and mood (grass day, sunset, cave, underwater, castle/haunted, etc.) — SMW's "one world = one mood" clarity.
7. **Juice:** squash/stretch, dust puffs, sparkles, bounce stars, coin-style collectible spin, bouncy pickups.

## What we add (the "more detail, more depth" request) without breaking the essence

- Richer multi-layer parallax, sunset/time-of-day variants, animated water/foliage, prop variety, dithering for sky bands only (never on characters).
- Extra highlight/shadow tone on tiles and props, tasteful ambient particles.
- Much larger customization (layered humanoid creator).

## What we must NOT do

- **No copying** of any Nintendo (or other) character, sprite, tile, logo, enemy, music, level, or signature motif. "Inspired by the essence" ≠ reuse. Specifically avoid clone-adjacent designs (no red-capped plumber, no green dinosaur with saddle, no eyes-on-hills, no shell-enemy/mushroom-enemy lookalikes, no question-block lookalike). Invent our own signature motifs (spring/coil/bounce-ring, "haven shards").
- No realistic/soft/painterly rendering, no pre-rendered "glossy 3D" look, no heavy noise, no photographic gradients, no pure-black fills, no tiny illegible detail, no mixed pixel scales.
- No cubes/blocks as characters.

## How the two production methods must serve this

- Hand-authored pixel art is the reference look.
- Any Blender/3D → 2D pipeline must be tuned to *imitate hand-pixeled SMW-style output*: flat/toon shading with 2–3 tones, strong dark outlines, small frames, stepped/limited animation timing, palette-locked colors, cleanup to remove 3D tells. If a 3D-rendered result looks like DKC-style glossy pre-rendered art rather than bold SMW-style sprites, it fails this page.
- Reference-study rule stays: take principles and craft lessons only.
