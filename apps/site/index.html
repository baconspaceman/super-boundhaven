A sharp 16-bit, side-scrolling platforming MMO where movement is the skill, the world is shared, and the hardest challenges are meant to be beaten as a team.

Why it’s different

Built around how it feels to move

Inspired by the bold, readable essence of classic 16-bit platformers, made original from the ground up. Here is what we are building, and honestly how far along each piece is.

  • Precision movement, real skill gap

    Momentum, run-jumps, slopes and bounce pads tuned for feel, with short coyote-time and jump-buffer windows. Easy to pick up, deep enough that mastery shows. How failure and checkpoints work is still being decided.

    Playable prototype

  • A real-time shared world

    Other players are visible as you play, and you physically bump, stomp and bounce off each other. Today that is one small shared playground; larger regions and scale are still to be tested.

    Playable prototype (small)

  • A big layered character creator

    A layered humanoid look, built from skin, hair, face, clothes, hats, back items and more. Pick a look in the prototype and everyone sees it.

    Playable prototype

  • Summonable mounts & Metroidvania exploration

    Many original rideable animals, summoned in the open world whenever you like. Some places need a particular mount, with secrets and hidden routes to find. Art exists; the mechanics are not built yet.

    Planned

  • Genuinely cooperative dungeons & raids

    Challenges that need coordinated bounces, switches and timing, so one player cannot simply carry everyone else. The stomp-bounce that makes it possible already works in the prototype.

    Planned

  • Player-made levels & weekly events

    Build and share your own levels. A featured level each week, plus races and time-attack events on familiar favorites.

    Later

  • Original powerups

    A growing toolbox of consumable powerups, all with their own designs and their own quirks.

    Planned

  • Gear that does something

    Functional equipment and loadouts that change how you play, plus tradeable items. Your base moveset still has to be enough to clear hard content with exceptional skill.

    Later

  • Secrets & Easter eggs

    Hidden routes, odd corners and subtle nods to the things we love, tucked away for the curious.

    Planned

              Co-op raids

              Some walls need a friend

              The hardest content is designed so that nobody can carry everyone else. Success takes coordinated bounces, switches and timing, with the whole team executing together.

              • Stomp-bounces let one player boost another over terrain no solo jump can clear.
              • Switches and timed sequences that only work when several people hit them together.
              • A difficulty ladder from friendly dungeons up to punishing raids: challenging, but never unfair. The hardest raids are planned for eight players, with smaller co-op rooms for two to four. All of it is still being designed.

              Development log

              How we build it

              We would rather show the work than hype it. Here is how the pieces are made, and an honest status for each.

              • The movement simulation

                One deterministic TypeScript simulation steps at 60 ticks per second and is shared by the browser and the server, so everyone runs the same movement code. It covers momentum, variable-height jumps, slopes, bounce pads and solid players. The gameplay reel above is this exact code, replaying scripted inputs.

                Working prototype

              • Netcode: prediction plus authority

                Your browser predicts your own movement immediately so it feels instant, while an authoritative server runs the real simulation, sends snapshots about twenty times a second, and corrects any drift. Other players are smoothed between snapshots. It has not been tested with large crowds yet.

                Working prototype, small scale

              • Art pipeline

                Characters, creatures, mounts and tiles are authored as layered pixel data, all original, with no third-party assets. Backdrops are rendered from procedural 3D scenes in Blender, then reduced to a locked palette so they sit with the hand-placed pixels. The aim is bold, readable, cheerful 16-bit. Much of it is still waiting for a human pixel-artist polish pass.

                In progress

              • Character creator

                A look is a compact 20-character code describing every layer choice. The same code drives the live preview, the network and the in-game sprite, and the server validates it. New options slot in without changing the creator screen.

                Playable prototype

              FAQ

              Questions, answered honestly

              Can I play it right now?

              There is an early prototype: a shared movement playground and a character creator. It is rough, it changes often and it is not the full game. The Play button opens it when a build is available.

              When does it launch?

              We have no launch date and will not guess at one. The roadmap above shows order and priority, not a schedule.

              Which platforms?

              Browser first. Steam later, with the goal of letting browser and Steam players share the same world. None of that cross-play exists yet.

              How much will it cost?

              Pricing and monetization have not been decided. We will say so plainly before anything is sold.

              Will there be mounts?

              Yes, that is the direction: many original rideable animals you can summon in the open world, and some places that need a particular one. The first four are a frog, a dinosaur, a flying dinosaur and a cheetah. What each one can do, and how you get them, is still being designed.

              Do I need an account?

              The prototype does not use accounts, and accounts and saved progress are not built yet. The waitlist is only for news.

              Is it a copy of another game?

              No. It is inspired by the bold, readable feel of classic 16-bit platformers, but the art, characters, mounts, movement tuning and world are our own originals.

              Is the art final?

              No. It is original and playable, and we like where it is going, but much of it is early and will get a polish pass. Regions marked Concept are rough sketches only.

              What happens to my email?

              It is stored only to send Super BoundHaven news, and you can ask to be removed at any time. Sign-ups may show as “not connected yet” while the waitlist backend is still being built.

              Is there gambling?

              No. The casino idea is a fictional story beat for a much later update. Real-money gambling is not part of the plan.
